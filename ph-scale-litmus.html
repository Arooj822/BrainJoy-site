How litmus works

Litmus is a dye extracted from lichens. Acids turn blue litmus paper red; bases turn red litmus paper blue. If neither strip changes, the substance is neutral. Litmus tells you acid-or-base, but not how strong — for that you need a pH value.

Why "no change" matters

A strip that keeps its color is still data. Litmus only flips color when a solution is clearly acidic or clearly basic, so a substance like distilled water leaves both strips unchanged. Litmus cannot tell you the exact pH or how close to neutral something is — for that you need indicator solution or a pH meter.
