Why Thomson needed a new model

In 1897, Thomson discovered the electron using cathode ray tubes. This proved atoms were not indivisible. Since the whole atom was neutral, Thomson proposed a "pudding" of positive charge with negative electrons embedded like plums — or chocolate chips — inside it.

Why the cookie analogy works

Both the cookie dough and Thomson's positive charge are continuous and spread out. The chocolate chips and electrons are discrete, embedded particles scattered throughout. It's a perfect visual match — tasty and memorable!
