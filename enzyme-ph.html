Why the peak?

An enzyme's active site is held in shape by ionic bonds and hydrogen bonds. At the optimum pH that shape fits its substrate perfectly. Move the pH and the charges on amino acids change, warping the active site — so the reaction slows.

Denaturation

Far enough from the optimum, the enzyme's shape unravels — it is denatured, and for many enzymes this change cannot be undone. Pepsin is built for stomach acid (optimum around pH 2) and slowly loses its shape in neutral conditions, while salivary amylase (optimum ~pH 7) keeps working for a while after swallowing and only denatures as the stomach's acid takes over.

Real bodies, real pH zones

Your digestive tract is a pH assembly line: mouth ~7, stomach ~2, small intestine ~8. Each enzyme evolved for its own zone, which is why digestion happens in stages instead of all at once.
